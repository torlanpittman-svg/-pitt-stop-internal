## What this means (assessment, not data)

- **Local exact-phrase demand for the four priority services is thin**: every restoration phrase is ≤ 10 searches/month
  in the two cities, and most insurance/vandalism/water phrases return no data at all. Google only reports volumes it can
  estimate; "—" is not proof of zero intent, but it rules out a keyword-volume-driven forecast.
- **Glass is the only cluster with measurable local volume** (auto glass repair near me 40, car window replacement 20),
  and it is also the cluster the shop does NOT fulfil in-house (glass is sourced/subcontracted; see fulfilment check).
  Paying $2–$11 a click for a subcontracted service is the weakest economic case in the set.
- **Overspray** has the clearest national demand pattern (210/mo, high competition) and the best in-house margin
  (paint-correction labor), so it is the best candidate for paid Search — but at ~10 local searches/month it will need
  a wider radius (contractor/fleet overspray incidents happen county-wide) and broader phrase/broad match with tight
  negatives, not exact-match cities.
- **Vandalism / break-in and water damage are event-driven** (a storm, a parking-garage break-in wave) and show up as
  spikes, not steady volume. These are better served by (a) always-on organic pages that rank when the event happens,
  (b) a small always-on Search campaign on broad themes with presence-based targeting, and (c) referral channels
  (insurance agents, glass shops, apartment complexes, parking operators) that the ad budget does not buy.
- **Budget feasibility** is therefore NOT established by keyword volume. The $600/month can plausibly buy ~60–150 clicks
  across the three clusters at the observed bid ranges; whether that yields 15 qualified leads/month depends on landing
  page conversion (unknown; no baseline) and on event frequency. Treat the Day-90 targets as a hypothesis to test with
  stop-loss rules, not as a plan the data supports.
